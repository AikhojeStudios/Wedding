Chapter I · Outer Banks Weddings

Wind, salt, and golden light.

The Outer Banks are made for weddings that feel free. Ceremonies on the sand in Duck or Nags Head, intimate vows on a soundside dock in Corolla, big beach-house weekends where the whole family stays under one roof. I photograph and film them the way they actually feel: windswept, sun-drenched, and a little bit wild.

My approach is editorial and documentary. I guide portraits when you want direction, then step back and let the day unfold, so your gallery holds the real moments as much as the posed ones.

Chapter II · Good to Know

Planning an Outer Banks wedding.

A few things I talk through with every Outer Banks couple. I also photograph weddings in Virginia Beach & Hampton Roads, Williamsburg, Richmond, Raleigh-Durham, Wilmington, and anywhere your day takes you.

  1. 01

    Sound side vs. ocean side

    The ocean faces east, so sunset light is softest on the sound side. Many couples hold the ceremony on the beach and take portraits by the sound at golden hour.

  2. 02

    Plan for the wind

    There is almost always a breeze off the water. Veils, hair, and florals look beautiful in it when you plan for it, and I build time into the day for it.

  3. 03

    Beach ceremony permits

    Each Outer Banks town sets its own rules for beach weddings. Check with your town, rental company, or planner early; I am happy to help you think through timing.

  4. 04

    Beach-house weddings

    Rental-home celebrations are an Outer Banks tradition. I photograph getting ready, the ceremony, and the reception all in one place, which leaves more time for portraits.
